Selected case studies.

Each framed as the challenge, my approach, and the measurable outcome.

01
Financial Modeling & Forecasting
~25%
Forecast inflation reconciled out across business units before plan lock

One plan, not three overlapping forecasts.

Challenge

The annual forecast pulled bottoms-up inputs from marketing, sales, and product. Because their initiatives targeted the same business outcomes, the same expected results were being counted more than once, inflating the consolidated plan by roughly 25%.

Approach

Owned the reconciliation across business units. Traced overlapping initiatives back to their source, worked with each team to separate what was genuinely incremental from what was double-counted, and rebuilt the forecast on a reconciled basis.

Outcome

Realigned the consolidated forecast before it became the plan of record, stripping out roughly 25% of double-counting inflation and giving leadership a single defensible number to plan against.

02
Market Sizing & Competitive Intelligence
$25B by 2030
Core market opportunity mapped

Sizing the market, and reading everyone competing in it.

Challenge

The strategy team had no maintained view of its addressable market and no consistent read on how the largest competitors were performing. Leadership needed a defensible, current picture to make decisions in a fast-changing category.

Approach

Built ADT's market-sizing view from third-party research and competitors' public investor materials, and stood up a repeatable quarterly system to analyze competitor performance on a true like-for-like basis, with quality control on every input.

Outcome

Gave executives a clear read on a core home-security opportunity growing from $19B to $25B by 2030, plus $20B+ in adjacencies.

03
Board & Executive Reporting
50% earlier
Monthly leadership review, with 25% less meeting time

Owning the two artifacts leadership decides from.

Challenge

The company's two highest-visibility reporting artifacts, the quarterly Board deck and the monthly leadership review, were high-stakes and slow. The Board deck allowed no room for error on an immovable timeline, and the monthly review landed too late to drive timely decisions.

Approach

Partnered across FP&A, finance, and department leads to own the quarterly Board deck end to end for 2+ years, validating 50+ metrics with senior leaders. Extended the same reporting model into a redesigned monthly leadership review, building the automation behind it.

Outcome

Monthly review now lands 50% earlier in the cycle with 25% less meeting time. Continued partnership with leadership and the team refined it further into a template-based structure, mirroring the Board deck format. The meeting now focuses more on insights and decisions instead of confirming numbers.
